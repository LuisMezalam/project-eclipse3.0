Core load equations

Convert the supplied aerodynamic data into dimensional forces and moments.

01 / Dynamic pressure & coefficient scales
q∞ = ½ρV∞²
Q = q∞S
F = Q CF
Mi = q∞S ℓi CMi
Q is the reference force scale. Each 3D moment coefficient uses its own declared length ℓᵢ before vector rotation. In 2D, panel tractions are integrated directly over contour × span.
02 / Surface traction integration
qΓ = −pg n̂out + τ t̂
dF = b qΓ ds
R = ∫Γ b qΓ ds
MO = ∫Γ (r−rO) × b qΓ ds
For a counterclockwise 2D contour with panel tangent t̂=(tₓ,tᵧ), n̂out=(tᵧ,−tₓ). For each panel: dA=b(−pgtᵧ+τtₓ)ds; dN=b(pgtₓ+τtᵧ)ds.
Section area modifier
(x/c, y/c) → (x/c, λAy/c)
Acs,new = λAAcs
The positive modifier scales the panel contour's thickness about the chord line while leaving chord fixed. Panel forces, moments, and the displayed enclosed area use that adjusted geometry with the supplied pressure/shear data. The flow field and coefficients are not recomputed.
03 / Body ↔ wind axes · 2D
[D, L]ᵀ = [[cosα, sinα], [−sinα, cosα]] [A, N]ᵀ
A = D cosα − L sinα
N = D sinα + L cosα
This declared rotation preserves force magnitude. It defines D=A cosα+N sinα and L=−A sinα+N cosα; angles are entered in degrees and converted internally to radians.
04 / Body → wind axes · 3D
[D, YW, L]ᵀ = CWB[A, YB, N]ᵀ
[Mx, My, Mz]Wᵀ = CWB[Mx, My, Mz]Bᵀ
CWB = [[cαcβ, sβ, sαcβ], [−cαsβ, cβ, −sαsβ], [−sα, 0, cα]]
Here cα=cosα, sα=sinα and likewise for β. This is the workbench's declared rotation order and sideslip sign. The matrix is orthogonal with determinant +1; force and dimensional moment vectors use the same rotation.
05 / Reference-point transport
MB = MO − (rB−rO) × R
2D: MB,z = MO,z − [(xB−xO)N − (yB−yO)A]
Transport the moment with the dimensional force and point displacement. Rotating force axes alone does not change the physical moment reference.
06 / Physical center of pressure
xcp/c = xO/c + (MO,z − yOA)/(cN)
rcp = (xcp, 0)
A line of action is reported only when the body-normal force N is sufficiently far from zero. This is a physical wrench result, not the centroid of pressure magnitude.
07 / Pressure-footprint intensity centroid
rI = ∫Γ r |pg| ds / ∫Γ |pg| ds
A nonnegative diagnostic for where pressure magnitude is concentrated. It excludes shear and signed cancellation; it is not a force line of action or a physical center of pressure.

General airfoil force relations

Local surface geometry and the global body-to-wind frame use separate angles.

Global frame angle
θ = α
L = N cosα − A sinα
D = N sinα + A cosα
The wind axes are the body axes rotated by angle of attack; perpendicularity means the normal-to-lift angle θ equals α. This geometric identity does not set the local airfoil slope.
Upper and lower local surface components
Upper: dA=b(pusinβu+τucosβu)ds
dN=b(−pucosβu+τusinβu)ds
Lower: dA=b(−plsinβl+τlcosβl)ds
dN=b(plcosβl+τlsinβl)ds
These formulas use each surface tangent from leading edge to trailing edge. The general outward-normal traction formula remains the preferred sign-safe rule for arbitrary panel winding.
Piecewise and tabulated data
Piecewise constant: F=b Σi Δsi(−pin̂i+τit̂i)
Trapezoid: A≈b Σj(fA,j+fA,j+1)Δsj/2
N≈b Σj(fN,j+fN,j+1)Δsj/2
Panel rows now accept endpoint pressure and shear. Equal endpoint values recover piecewise-constant loads; differing endpoints are linearly interpolated over the segment, so force integration is trapezoidal.
Aerodynamic coefficients
CA=A/(q∞Sref), CN=N/(q∞Sref)
CD=D/(q∞Sref), CL=L/(q∞Sref)
Cp=(p−p∞)/q∞, Cf=τ/q∞
Use the reference area specified by the problem, usually planform area. A panel pressure/shear distribution is dimensionalized over b and normalized by Sref only when forming coefficients.
Uniform-pressure closure check
∮S p0 n̂out dS = 0
R=0 for uniform p0 on a closed body (τ=0)
A nonzero residual indicates a missing face, inconsistent contour closure or normals, or mixed pressure references. Absolute pressure or one consistent gauge reference may be used on the complete closed boundary.

Flow estimates & CFD surface reduction

Generated 2D fields and imported 3D triangle data use separate, declared model assumptions.

2D Hess–Smith pressure estimate
V∞·n̂i + Σjσjun,ij + γΣjuv,n,ij = 0
Vt,i = V∞·t̂i + Σσjun,t,ij + γΣuv,t,ij
Cp,i = 1 − (Vt,i/V∞)²
A constant source strength per panel and one circulation strength enforce no-penetration; a sharp trailing-edge Kutta condition closes the system. This is an inviscid, incompressible, attached-flow estimate. Refine the contour and compare against validated data.
Approximate wall shear
Rex = ρ|Vt|s/μ
Cf,x ≈ 0.664/√Rex (laminar)
Cf,x ≈ 0.0592/Rex0.2 (turbulent)
Cf = sign(Vt)(Vt/V∞)²Cf,x
The smooth-flat-plate correlation uses a simple transition Reynolds threshold and a leading-edge distance proxy. It omits pressure-gradient and separation effects; treat Cᶠ as a rough estimate, not a boundary-layer solution.
3D triangle traction mapping
n̂ = ((r₂−r₁)×(r₃−r₁))/|(r₂−r₁)×(r₃−r₁)|
t = −p n̂ + [τ−(τ·n̂)n̂]
F = Σf ∫f t dA
MO = Σf ∫f (r−rO)×t dA
Vertex pressure and shear are linearly interpolated on each flat triangle and integrated with a three-point degree-2 rule. The vertex order sets the outward normal. Constant-face rows are treated as repeated vertex values.
CFD surface CSV and VTK import
CSV accepts 21 values per triangle for vertex fields or 13 for face-constant fields. ASCII legacy VTK DATASET POLYDATA accepts POINTS, POLYGONS, SCALARS and VECTORS (or 1/3-component FIELD arrays); fan triangulation preserves source polygon cell values. Map one scalar pressure and one three-component shear vector. Coordinates are converted from the selected VTK unit to metres. Binary/XML VTK, unstructured volumes, extra VTK cell types, and solving the flow are unsupported.
Pressure/shear wrench split
Fp=∫S−p n̂ dS; Fτ=∫SτtdS
MO=∫S(r−rO)×dF
The diagnostic reports pressure and tangential wall-shear contributions separately, then shows their sum. Imported shear is projected onto each triangle's tangent plane; pressure is a gauge pressure unless coefficient fields are selected.
Surface topology diagnostics
Triangle edges are welded by coordinate tolerance and checked for open boundaries, edges shared by more than two faces, inconsistent shared-edge winding, duplicate triangles, and connected components. Open boundaries can be valid for a surface patch. These checks do not test skewness, aspect ratio, CFD residuals, conservation, or solution accuracy.

Common special reductions

Useful checks and simplified forms for familiar airfoil setups.

Small-angle interpretation
sinα≈α; cosα≈1 (α in radians)
L≈N−αA; D≈A+αN
Use the exact rotation for reported results. This approximation is for interpretation only when α is demonstrably small.
Flat plate with negligible thickness
βu=βl=0
A=b∫τuds+b∫τlds+Aedge
N=b∫plds−b∫puds+Nedge
Pressure difference mainly contributes normal force; skin friction mainly contributes axial force. Include leading/trailing edge faces if they have finite area.
Symmetric loading at α=0
pu(s)=pl(s), τu(s)=τl(s), βu(s)=−βl(s)
⇒ N=0 and L=0; D=A
Geometry symmetry by itself is insufficient. The loading must also be symmetric, and this cancellation applies at zero angle of attack.
Symmetric wedge aligned with flow
α=0; equal face pressure p, shear τ, slope ±δ, length s
N=0; A=2bs(p sinδ+τ cosδ)
L=0; D=A
Sloped pressure normals can create drag even with equal pressure on both wedge faces.
One segment with local slope β=α
dA=b(p sinα+τ cosα)ds
dN=b(−p cosα+τ sinα)ds
dL=−bp ds; dD=bτ ds
This cancellation holds only on a segment whose local tangent is aligned with the drag axis under the manual's convention. It is not an identity for the whole curved airfoil.

Definitions & coordinate rules

Use these meanings consistently when preparing coefficients, panel tables, and moment references.

Symbols and units
ρ: fluid density (kg/m³); μ: dynamic viscosity (Pa·s); V∞: freestream speed (m/s); q∞: dynamic pressure (Pa); S: reference area (m²); Acs: enclosed airfoil cross-section area (m²); λA: positive section thickness/area modifier; c: chord (m); b: full span (m); Cₚ: pressure coefficient; Cᶠ: signed skin-friction coefficient. Forces are N and moments N·m. α is angle of attack; β is sideslip in 3D and local tangent angle in the 2D surface formulas (context determines which). Input angles are in degrees.
Force and moment notation
In body axes, 2D A is axial and N is normal; in wind axes D is drag and L is lift. The 3D body force is (A,YB,N); the wind force is (D,YW,L). MO is a physical moment about origin O, and MB is the same wrench transported to point B.
Panel contour coordinates
Panel vertices are ordered around the solid and entered as (x/c,y/c). Each row defines a directed straight segment. pg is gauge pressure in Pa; τ is signed wall shear in Pa along the segment's start-to-end tangent. The entered 2D section load is extruded over the configured full span b. In the panel route, λA vertically scales y/c about the chord line; cross-section area and thickness scale linearly while chord remains fixed.
Reference points and lengths
2D origin and target coordinates are (x/c,y/c). 3D points are entered as (x/c,y/b,z/c). The 3D moment scales ℓₓ, ℓᵧ, ℓ𝓏 are independent user inputs. Moment coefficients are dimensionalized using these lengths before rotating or transporting the moment vector.

Set rules & model assumptions

These rules define what an input means and when a result is valid.

Panel input rules
Enter consecutive vertices counterclockwise so the right-hand panel normal points outward. p₁, τ₁ belong to the directed panel start and p₂, τ₂ to its end. Positive shear follows start-to-end; for the upper face of a CCW contour this direction runs trailing edge to leading edge. Values may be Pa or Cₚ/Cᶠ as selected. Use every exposed base/edge face and one consistent absolute or gauge pressure reference. Refine near rapid gradients. The positive section-area modifier scales y/c and the contour thickness, but reuses the same supplied surface values.
Coefficient input rules
Choose whether the supplied forces are body-axis or wind-axis coefficients, and enter a consistent reference area. For 2D, the moment coefficient is about the declared input point and uses q∞ S cₘ. For 3D, supply the separate moment lengths, then rotate and transport the resulting dimensional wrench.
Beam-transfer rules
The optional estimate uses a symmetric straight wing and a 2D section load mirrored across the centerline. Let Fwing be the total full-wing component and s run root-to-tip over [0,b/2]. The semispan shear is Fwing/2. Uniform and elliptical load families are the only selectable shapes; a total 3D wrench is insufficient to infer a spanwise field.
Scope & validation rules
The 2D solver estimates incompressible potential-flow pressure on a closed contour with a sharp trailing-edge Kutta condition, then estimates wall shear using local flat-plate correlations. It does not model pressure-gradient boundary layers, transition, separation, compressibility, shocks, or profile drag. The separate low-speed lifting-line screen estimates induced drag from declared inputs but does not generate a finite-wing pressure field. The CFD route post-processes mapped CSV or ASCII legacy VTK POLYDATA surface fields; it does not solve Navier–Stokes or interpret volume meshes. Beam stress and deflection are outside scope.

Standard scenario guide

Each common airfoil case maps to the available reduction, polar, condition, or screening tools.

ScenarioHow to represent it
1 · Zero angle of attackSet α=0. The wind/body check should give D=A and L=N; lift is not automatically zero.
2 · Positive or negative αEnter signed α once. The exact rotation handles its sign; do not reverse force signs a second time.
3 · Thin flat plateUse the coefficient route for a known resultant, or a thin closed panel contour with both broad faces and edge faces; set local face slopes near zero.
4 · Symmetric section, α=0, symmetric loadingEnter mirrored geometry and matching pressure/shear distributions. The normal resultant should cancel within discretization error.
5 · Symmetric geometry, nonzero αKeep the geometry symmetric but enter the actual possibly asymmetric upper/lower loading. Geometry alone does not justify cancelling forces.
6 · Symmetric wedge aligned with flowBuild the upper/lower wedge panels, set α=0, and enter the stated pressure and signed shear on each face.
7 · One segment with local β=αSet that segment’s geometry slope to α and enter its own pressure/shear. Apply the condition to that segment only; other panel slopes remain independent.
8 · Pressure-only / inviscid dataSet every τ endpoint to zero and supply the pressure field. Inviscid does not mean zero pressure force.
9 · Shear-only dataSet every pressure endpoint to zero and enter signed τ along each directed panel tangent.
10 · Blunt base or trailing-edge faceAdd a panel for the finite face and its pressure/shear. Do not omit exposed surfaces.
11 · Uniform pressure on a closed bodySet one common pressure at all endpoints and τ=0. The net force must cancel; use this as a geometry/sign check.
12 · Piecewise-constant tractionUse identical start/end pressure and shear on each panel; the polygonal integration is exact for that discretization.
13 · Tabulated or experimental distributionsImport eight-column endpoint data or enter it directly. Select Pa or Cₚ/Cᶠ first; coefficients are scaled by q∞. Refine stations near leading-edge gradients and discontinuities.
14 · Known A,N or D,LChoose body or wind coefficient input respectively, set the matching coefficients and α, then read the transformed components.
15 · Aerodynamic coefficientsUse q∞, the specified reference area, and coefficient basis. The calculator dimensionalizes the provided data; it does not predict the coefficients.
16 · Moment about a selected pointSet the source and target reference coordinates. The wrench is transported with the cross-product relation; force alone does not define a moment.
17 · Compressible, separated, or reversed flowSupply the resulting pressure and signed shear data. The force equations remain valid; this workbench does not compute those flow distributions.
18 · Flight-state comparisonEnter or fill atmospheric state, then inspect Mach and chord Reynolds number before selecting a coefficient dataset or interpreting the 2D estimate.
19 · Measured coefficient polarImport α, CL, CD, and quarter-chord Cm. Interpolation is bounded to the data range and tagged with its Re/Mach condition.
20 · Finite-wing screeningUse the lifting-line estimate with full span, reference area, section slope, zero-lift angle, and span efficiency. Treat it as low-speed and unswept.
21 · Aircraft longitudinal stabilityEnter complete-configuration CLα and nose-up-positive Cmα, then declare xref and xcg to estimate neutral point and static margin.
22 · Panel / mesh refinementRecord successive runs in the convergence log and compare force/moment changes while holding conditions and references fixed.
3D wing / spanwise effectsUse the CFD triangle route when a 3D pressure/shear surface field is available, or the 3D coefficient route for a known total wrench. The beam estimate still requires its symmetric analytic spanwise-load assumption.

Student and research workflow

Use these steps to make a result interpretable and repeatable by someone else.

01 / Qualify the source data
Record airfoil or vehicle geometry, test/solver source, angle convention, Reynolds and Mach conditions, coefficient reference area, moment reference, pressure basis, shear axes, and surface orientation. Enter the citation and setup in the Study record before saving the case.
02 / Verify the reduction
Check dynamic pressure and units first. For 2D panel data, confirm contour closure and counterclockwise order; uniform pressure on a closed contour is a useful zero-result check. For 3D data, inspect outward triangle normals. Compare force and moment trends under refinement, then independently check at least one result against a hand calculation or trusted reference.
03 / Archive and compare
Save the complete case JSON to reopen the same inputs and imported data; reopening recalculates with the active tool build rather than freezing the calculation engine. Export results JSON for the calculation record; export a polar load-sweep CSV to compare the imported angle rows at the current q∞, area, and chord. No export corrects a dataset for mismatched conditions.

Optional semispan beam equations

The analysis view calculates these only for an active 2D load and the selected span shape.

12 / Spanwise line-load families
Uniform: w(s)=1
Elliptic: w(s)=√[1−(2s/b)²]
qsemi(s) = (Fwing/2) w(s) / ∫0b/2w(u)du
s is distance outboard from the root. The normalization makes the load integrated on one semispan equal Fwing/2 for either shape.
13 / Root shear and bending
Vroot = Fwing/2
Mroot = ∫0b/2s qsemi(s) ds
Uniform: Mroot=Fwingb/8
Elliptic: Mroot=Fwingb/(3π)
The mean arm relative to semispan root shear is b/4 for uniform loading and 2b/(3π) for elliptic loading.
14 / Root action components and section couple
Va=A/2; Vn=N/2
Ma=Vnb k; Mn=−Vab k
k = ¼ (uniform), 2/(3π) (elliptic)
Ms=−Mc/4/2
With body axes a×s=n, the section moment direction a×n is −s. The minus sign in Ms preserves this right-hand mapping for the two mirrored semispans.
15 / Internal shear and bending diagrams
V(s)=∫sb/2qsemi(u)du
M(s)=∫sb/2(u−s)qsemi(u)du
These are analytic-family load diagrams under the assumed span model, not structural stress or deformation predictions. A user-supplied section load shape is held fixed along the wing.
